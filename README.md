# ✈️ Aviation AI Communication
### AI-Powered Passenger Communication for Airline Disruptions

An AI-assisted passenger communication workflow that combines **prompt engineering, source-based verification, and human review** to improve the accuracy, clarity, and consistency of airline disruption communications.

---

## 🎯 Project Overview

Airline service teams must communicate changing flight information clearly and consistently across multiple channels. Incorrect timings, unsupported benefit claims, and inconsistent updates can create passenger confusion and reduce trust.

This project explores how AI can support airline passenger communication through structured prompting, factual verification, audience adaptation, and human oversight using fictional flight scenarios.

## 🚀 Key Features

- 📧 **Multi-Channel Communication:** Draft passenger emails, announcements, SMS messages, and service desk responses.
- 🧠 **Six Prompting Strategies:** Compare zero-shot, context and constraints, role, few-shot, structured-output, and audience-based prompting.
- 🔎 **Factual Verification:** Check AI-generated responses against supplied flight facts and policies.
- 🔄 **Disruption Updates:** Update passenger communications when operational timings change.
- 🌐 **Bilingual Consistency:** Review English–Hindi email pairs for consistency of meaning and factual details.
- ♻️ **Reusable Prompt Template:** Test a reusable passenger email prompt on a separate fictional flight scenario.

## ⚙️ Project Workflow

1. **Establish the facts** — Identify flight details, applicable fictional policies, and missing information.
2. **Generate drafts** — Use structured prompts to produce passenger communications.
3. **Evaluate outputs** — Review accuracy, completeness, clarity, tone, and consistency.
4. **Verify and correct** — Flag unsupported claims and correct discrepancies against the supplied information.
5. **Update communications** — Apply revised operational timings consistently across relevant messages.
6. **Review translations** — Check English–Hindi messages for consistent facts and meaning.
7. **Test reusability** — Apply the email prompt template to a different fictional flight scenario.

## 🧪 Verification Scenarios

| Scenario | Verification Objective |
|---|---|
| ✈️ **SB204 — 2:00 PM scheduled, 4:30 PM expected** | Calculate the 2-hour-30-minute delay and check eligibility under the fictional refreshment voucher policy. |
| 🔄 **SB204 — Expected departure revised to 5:15 PM** | Replace the earlier estimate and use the revised 4:15 PM passenger update. |
| ❓ **Unconfirmed gate, refund, or hotel details** | Identify missing information without inventing answers or assuming eligibility. |
| ⚠️ **Guaranteed departure wording** | Preserve the distinction between an expected departure time and a confirmed guarantee. |
| 🛫 **SB310 — 10:00 AM scheduled, 11:15 AM expected** | Verify the 1-hour-15-minute delay falls below the supplied two-hour voucher eligibility threshold. |

> **Note:** All flight scenarios and policies are fictional and are used exclusively for educational purposes.

## 🤖 Responsible AI & Human Oversight

AI is used as a drafting and review assistant, not as an independent source of operational truth.

The workflow emphasises:

- ✅ Checking generated content against supplied source information.
- ✅ Identifying unsupported claims and missing details.
- ✅ Avoiding invented benefits, procedures, and guarantees.
- ✅ Reviewing revised timings across all affected communications.
- ✅ Maintaining human responsibility for final approval.

## ⚠️ Scope & Limitations

- Uses fictional flight information and classroom policies.
- Does not connect to live airline systems or operational databases.
- Does not send messages to actual passengers.
- Does not independently confirm real-time flight status, passenger entitlements, or airline procedures.
- Requires human verification before any communication could be approved for real-world use.

## 💡 Key Learning

**Effective AI-assisted aviation communication depends on more than generating fluent text.** Clear prompts, reliable source information, consistent updates, and human verification are essential for producing useful and responsible passenger communications.
